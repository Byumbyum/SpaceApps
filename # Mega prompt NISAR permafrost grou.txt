## 1. Goal

Build an open-source interactive web app that uses NASA-ISRO NISAR radar data to show ground movement in Arctic Alaska, explains what it may mean for permafrost, and shows which road and pipeline sections sit near observed ground motion.

**Project objective (use this wording in the app):**
"We identify and explain radar-observed ground changes that may indicate emerging permafrost-related hazards, and show clearly which observations are trustworthy."

**Audience:** policymakers and the general public, including older users. Plain language, large readable type, no jargon without a one-line explanation.

**Novelty (what the app adds):** radar detecting permafrost change is established science. The app's contribution is an accessible, evidence-aware tool that brings in new NISAR observations and shows which observations are reliable enough to justify a closer look.

## 2. Science background the code must respect

- **InSAR** compares the phase of two radar images taken from the same orbit position at different times. Movement along the line of sight (LOS) shifts the phase: $\Delta\phi = \frac{4\pi}{\lambda}\Delta r$. NISAR L-band has $\lambda \approx 24$ cm, so one fringe ≈ 12 cm of LOS motion.
- InSAR measures **displacement, not thaw**. Seasonal frost heave, soil-water change, non-permafrost movement and artefacts can all appear as motion.
- Convert LOS to vertical only where motion is plausibly vertical: $d_{vert} \approx d_{LOS}/\cos\theta$ (θ = local incidence angle from product metadata). Do not apply on steep slopes.
- **Seasonal vs permanent:** intact ground sinks in summer and rises at freeze-up. Report (a) each summer's sinking amplitude and (b) the year-to-year residual at comparable dates separately. Never call a downward trend "permanent ice loss" as a fact; call it "consistent with" ice loss.
- InSAR values are **relative** to a reference point and date. Always store and display the reference.
- **Snow ruins the signal.** Thaw-season analysis uses snow-free pairs only (roughly June–September).
- **ADDT** (accumulated degree-days of thaw) = running yearly sum of $\max(T_{air,2m}, 0)$ from ERA5-Land, reset each year. Stefan relation: thaw depth/seasonal sinking $\propto \sqrt{ADDT}$ (basis of the ReSALT method). Use it only as an **association test**. The 2024 Dalton-area study (Sadeghi Chorsi, Meyer and Dixon) found measured thaw depth correlated weakly with ADDT (R² = 0.42) at the CALM site; soil moisture, vegetation, topography, snow and ground ice also matter.

### NISAR data status (as of 14 Nov 2026, from ASF)
- 17 Jun 2026: PROVISIONAL calibrated products begin.
- **27 Jul – 10 Aug 2026: permanent instrument data gap** (mid-thaw season). No 12-day pair can span it; time series must show the gap explicitly.
- 21 Oct 2026: VALIDATED acquisitions start (after freeze-up, so not useful for thaw). 6 Nov 2026: VALIDATED L0–L2 products released. Pre-21-Oct data will be reprocessed in 2027.
- Therefore the only NISAR thaw season available is **summer 2026, PROVISIONAL, with a gap**. Label it as such everywhere.
- GUNW (unwrapped interferogram) is posted at **80 m**, 12-day nearest-neighbour co-pol pairs. Its ionosphere, troposphere and solid-earth-tide correction layers are included but **not applied by default**: apply them in the pipeline.
- Check the ASF NISAR known-issues list before trusting interferograms (high-latitude ionosphere is a concern).
- **S-band is not available over Alaska.** Joint L+S data covers India, Antarctica, Greenland and selected sites only.

## 5. Data pipeline (offline, Python)

Language: Python 3.11. Suggested libraries: `asf_search`, `earthaccess`, `xarray`, `rioxarray`, `h5py`, `numpy`, `scipy`, `geopandas`, `shapely`, `pyproj`, `rasterio`, `cdsapi` (ERA5-Land), `osmnx` or Geofabrik extracts, `rio-cogeo`. Use EPSG:3338 (Alaska Albers) for analysis, EPSG:4326/3857 for web output.

### 5.1 Datasets (MVP)

| Dataset | Source | Use |
|---|---|---|
| NISAR GUNW (L2, 80 m, 12-day pairs) | ASF Vertex / Earthdata (`NISAR_L2_GUNW`) | Summer 2026 ground motion |
| OPERA DISP-S1 (Sentinel-1, 30 m, Jul 2016–now) | ASF DAAC | Multi-year baseline and cross-check |
| ERA5-Land (2 m air temperature, total precipitation) | Copernicus Climate Data Store | ADDT; rain factor |
| ESA CCI Permafrost (permafrost fraction, ~1 km) | CEDA | Interpretation index I |
| MODIS snow cover (daily, 500 m) | NSIDC | Snow exclusion |
| JRC Global Surface Water (30 m) | JRC | Water exclusion |
| Alaska fire perimeters | Alaska Interagency Coordination Center | Fire history; Anaktuvuk outline |
| OpenStreetMap roads and pipeline | Geofabrik | Segments for monitoring priority |
| Sentinel-2 / HLS true colour | Copernicus / LP DAAC | Visual context basemap |

Later: NISAR GCOV, ICESat-2, ALOS PALSAR, ArcticDEM, Alaska DEC Contaminated Sites database, community facilities (lagoons, clinics, runways).

### 5.2 Steps

1. **Common grid:** 100 m cells in EPSG:3338 over the MVP box. Every output is per cell or per road segment.
2. **DISP-S1 baseline:** download frames covering the box; read displacement, reference date/point and correction layers; resample to grid; record temporal coherence.
3. **NISAR GUNW:** search summer 2026 pairs over the box; for each pair apply ionosphere, troposphere and solid-earth-tide corrections; mask with coherence threshold and connected components; re-reference to a documented stable area; resample to grid. Mark the 27 Jul–10 Aug gap.
4. **Cross-check:** compare NISAR and DISP-S1 over shared date ranges; store per-cell agreement.
5. **Per-cell metrics:** each summer's sinking amplitude; year-to-year residual at comparable dates; fitted long-term rate $v$ with uncertainty from $d(t) = v\,t + A\sqrt{ADDT_y(t)} + c$ (report fit R² separately, do not use it in any index).
6. **Exclusions and factors:** compute every factor in section 6 per cell per date.
7. **Road segments:** split OSM highway and pipeline lines into ~1 km segments; buffer (e.g. 100 m each side); compute segment anomaly and z-score (section 6.3).
8. **Export:** Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs or PMTiles for map layers; compact JSON (or Parquet → JSON) for per-cell/segment time series and factor values; a `manifest.json` listing layers, dates, sources, reference point, processing version and product status (PROVISIONAL/VALIDATED).

### 5.3 Demo data mode

Ship a `demo/` dataset generator that produces synthetic but realistic layers and time series for the MVP box so the frontend works before real data is processed. **Every screen must show a visible "Demo data" banner when demo data is loaded.** Never mix demo and real data silently.

## 6. Scoring (config-driven)

All scoring lives in `scoring.config.json` (or YAML) and a pure-function module `scoring/` with unit tests. Changing weights, thresholds, transforms or factors must require editing config only. The UI reads the same config to render the "Why?" panel.

### 6.1 Hard exclusions (checked first; pixel greyed out with its reason)
- Mostly open water (JRC occurrence > 50%)
- Snow on either radar date (MODIS)
- Outside the permafrost map
- Coherence below a minimum or unwrapping error mask set

### 6.2 Two separate indices (neither is a probability)

**Measurement quality index Q:** is this displacement value trustworthy?
Factors (each mapped to 0–1 by a configurable transform): coherence (linear rescale 0.3→0, 0.7→1), share of valid pairs, reference stability, temporal consistency between overlapping pairs, magnitude of applied corrections (bigger = lower), agreement with DISP-S1 on shared dates.

**Thaw interpretation index I:** how well do independent lines of evidence support a permafrost-thaw explanation?
Factors: permafrost fraction (ESA CCI, used directly), thaw timing (e.g. $1 - e^{-ADDT/50}$), persistence of sinking between comparable seasons, fire history / ground-ice context, agreement with field data where available, recent-rain penalty ($e^{-r/r_0}$, window and $r_0$ in config).

**Combination (both indices):**
$$\text{Index} = \prod_i \max(f_i, \varepsilon)^{w_i}, \qquad \sum_i w_i = 1$$
True zeros are handled as exclusions before this step; ε (e.g. 0.01) is a floor. Bands (config): high ≥ 0.75, medium 0.50–0.75, low < 0.50.

Supported transform types in config: `identity`, `linear_rescale(min,max)`, `exp_decay(scale)`, `one_minus_exp(scale)`, `inverse(scale)`, `boolean`. Each factor entry: `id`, `label` (plain English), `source_dataset`, `transform`, `params`, `weight`, `index` (Q or I), `explanation` (one sentence shown in the UI).

### 6.3 Monitoring priority (replaces any "infrastructure risk" score)
For road/pipeline segment $k$:
$$a_k = \bar v_{segment} - \tilde v_{reference}, \qquad z_k = \frac{a_k}{\sigma_k}$$
$\bar v_{segment}$ = mean motion of valid cells in the segment buffer; $\tilde v_{reference}$ = median of nearby reference ground (configurable ring, e.g. 300–800 m, excluding infrastructure); $\sigma_k$ = combined uncertainty. Priority bands by |z| and segment-median Q (config). Label: "Monitoring priority: higher / moderate / lower". Tooltip: "Near observed ground motion; may warrant closer investigation. Not a structural safety rating."

### 6.4 Long-term context panel (no score)
Static, cited content: IPCC AR6 (high confidence thaw releases carbon, low confidence in amount; 3–41 GtC per °C by 2100, excluding abrupt thaw and fire); tipping-point research describes most of the feedback as threshold-free, with local abrupt-thaw tipping possible. Wording: "self-reinforcing feedback", never "runaway". State: "Our maps measure ground motion only, not carbon."

## 7. App specification

### 7.1 Stack
React + TypeScript + Vite. Map: MapLibre GL JS v5 with globe projection (or CesiumJS if 3D terrain is needed). Charts: a light library (e.g. Recharts or Observable Plot). State: Zustand or Redux Toolkit. Paintbrush selection: draw onto a raster mask aligned to the 100 m grid (or H3 cells), brush size adjustable. All data loaded from static files (no backend needed for MVP).

### 7.2 Layout (free-roam mode, from the team doc)
- **Centre:** rotatable 3D globe. Everything greyed out except the display region (50–73°N, 124–176°W). Zooming into the MVP box reveals data layers.
- **Left toolbar 1 (cursor tools):** Navigate · Paint to select area (scalable brush) · Paint to deselect (scalable brush). Only data within the selected area and selected timeframe is analysed.
- **Left toolbar 2 (timeframe):** start and end year; season multi-select (with the restrictions in section 3).
- **Right sidebar (scrollable analysis):**
  - Feature selector (multi-select): Ground motion (NISAR), Ground motion (Sentinel-1), Air temperature / ADDT, Measurement quality Q, Thaw interpretation I. Band filter: L-band (S-band disabled over Alaska).
  - Charts: each selected feature over time (median + error band + control-tundra line; NISAR summer 2026 points marked PROVISIONAL; data gap shaded).
  - Geographic heatmap of the selected feature for the selected area.
  - Infrastructure exposure: list and map of road/pipeline segments by monitoring priority.
  - Index summary for the selected area: share of pixels in each Q band and I band, plus medians (never an average of scores).
  - "Why?" panel: factor bars for a clicked pixel or the selected area, weakest factor highlighted ("Limited by: low radar coherence").
  - Long-term context panel (section 6.4).
  - "How to interpret this data" quick guide (section 7.5).
- **Map styling:** colour = motion rate (diverging, blue stable → red sinking, colour-blind safe); opacity = Q; excluded pixels hatched grey with reason on hover. Toggle: "Show high-quality data only".

### 7.3 Core visuals (MVP, must ship)
1. Ground-motion map (NISAR and Sentinel-1 layers, Q as opacity).
2. Time series with quality (Sentinel-1 2016–now + NISAR 2026, error bands, gap shown, control line).
3. Highway exposure overlay (segments by monitoring priority with uncertainty).

### 7.4 Story mode (built on free roam)
Scripted chapters; the camera flies to each location and shows short text. Each chapter has a "What we measured / what research says" split.
1. **The big picture loop** (show as a 7-station strip with labels):
   1 Human activity — *Published research* · 2 Warming — *Published research + our association test* · 3 Ground moves — **We measure** · 4 Wildfire — *Published case study* · 5 Infrastructure exposure — **We map** · 6 Cost and health — *Published research* · 7 Carbon feedback — *Published research* (feeds back to 2).
2. **Zoom to the northern Dalton corridor:** highway and pipeline on frozen ground.
3. **How radar measures movement:** simple animation of two passes and a phase shift; one fringe ≈ 12 cm.
4. **A decade of Sentinel-1:** time series for control tundra vs oilfield and highway.
5. **NISAR summer 2026:** first look, labelled PROVISIONAL, gap explained.
6. **Anaktuvuk River fire (published research):** 2007 fire burned ~1,039 km²; InSAR found up to 8 cm more thaw-season sinking afterwards (Liu et al., 2014); later lidar found additional sinking in <1% of the study area by 2021, indicating stabilisation, not a return to the original state.
7. **Monitoring priority along the highway.**
8. **Why it matters (published context):** ~70% of permafrost-domain infrastructure in high-thaw-potential areas by 2050 (Hjort et al., 2022); $37–51B projected Alaska building and road damage by mid-century; ~170,000 people in 192 Alaskan communities live in permafrost zones (Belfer Center); long-term carbon context.
9. **How to explore yourself:** hand-off to free roam.

### 7.5 "How to interpret this data" guide (content)
- Ground motion is measured relative to a stable reference; summer sinking and autumn rise are normal.
- A downward trend over years is consistent with ground-ice loss but can have other causes.
- Brightness changes every summer with normal thaw, rain, wet snow and plants; it is not proof of permafrost loss.
- Faded areas have lower measurement quality; hatched areas were excluded (reason on hover).
- Monitoring priority is not a safety rating.
- Thresholds and weights are the team's choices, shown in the "Why?" panel.

### 7.6 Wording rules (enforce in all UI copy)
| Never | Use |
|---|---|
| NISAR detects permafrost thaw | NISAR measures surface change that, with context data, can indicate permafrost degradation |
| The pipeline is at critical risk | These sections are near observed ground motion and may warrant closer investigation |
| Warmer summers cause the sinking | We test whether seasonal motion is associated with accumulated summer warmth |
| 30 years of thaw | A historical radar baseline combined with new NISAR observations |
| The burn scar recovered | Published evidence shows substantial post-fire stabilisation |
| Tipping-point risk score | Long-term context from published research |
| Confidence (as a probability) | Measurement quality index / thaw interpretation index |

### 7.7 Accessibility and UX
Minimum 16px body text, 44px touch targets, WCAG AA contrast, colour-blind-safe palettes, keyboard navigation, reduced-motion option for camera flights, works at 1280px desktop and degrades gracefully to tablet.
